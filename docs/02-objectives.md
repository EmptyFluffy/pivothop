# PivotHop — Objectives

*What we're actually building toward, in order. Read after the overview.*

---

## Business objective

Build a career-navigation instrument for anyone with transferable skills — any profession, mapped by skills rather than titles — monetized by growing into the **adjacent-talent job board** (employers flag roles open to adjacent candidates and pay to post and match; concierge introductions are the board's manual V0). The goal: enough revenue by month 12 to be Carlos's second income stream — not a startup, not a lifestyle app, a real small business that compounds through trust and data nobody else has.

**Not the objective:** raise venture capital. Reach scale. Serve every career-changer everywhere. Compete with LinkedIn.

## Product objective (V0)

Ship an honest, working instrument that produces defensible per-role adjacency data for 8-16 architect-to-adjacent-field routes, and that generates:
- At least 500 unique architects engaging with the tool in the first 90 days
- At least 100 email captures from the report modal
- At least 3 hand-matched employer introductions attempted
- At least 1 successful placement or paid pilot within 6 months

If these targets aren't hit, the concept doesn't have product-market fit at the boutique scale attempted. See kill criteria doc.

## Positioning objective

Own the category **"career navigation instrument"** — globally by product, vertical by vertical in mindshare. When anyone Googles "how to pivot from [their profession]," or asks in their profession's subreddit, or asks a senior colleague — PivotHop should be the answer that surfaces, and it should surface *because* it's actually useful, not because it paid for the placement. The first mindshare battle is the launch vertical (architecture), where the founder's credibility is native; each subsequent vertical repeats the same playbook on the same instrument.

Positioning success signals:
- Being cited unprompted in r/Architects and r/architecture threads
- Being recommended by at least one AEC educator (Novatr, ThinkParametric, PAACADEMY, How to Rhino, Oliver Thomas) within 6 months
- Ranking in top 5 organic results for "architect career change" and 2-3 similar queries within 9 months
- Being described by users as "the honest one" or "the one that isn't trying to sell you a bootcamp"

## Reputation objective

Build Carlos's own reputation in parallel with PivotHop's. The founder-practitioner identity is the single most defensible asset the brand has. Every article, every Reddit comment, every LinkedIn post, every conference appearance builds equity in both Carlos's professional brand *and* PivotHop's category authority. These are not separable.

By month 12, Carlos should be:
- Recognizable in r/Architects as "the architect who built the pivot tool"
- Cited by name in at least one industry publication or podcast
- Invited to speak at or contribute to at least one AEC education program

## Voice objective

Establish the deadpan-editorial-honest register as the brand's uncopyable voice moat. By month 6, another AI-generated career-tech landing page should be identifiable within 30 seconds as *not* PivotHop, because it sounds like every other career-tech landing page. PivotHop should sound like nothing else in the category.

This is a moat competitors can't easily copy because most career-tech companies are structurally incapable of writing this way — their legal, marketing, and investor incentives all point toward hype language.

## Business model objective

**Not this:** freemium tiers, ads, sponsored content, resume writing upsells, bootcamp affiliate revenue, LinkedIn Learning partnerships.

**This:** the adjacent-talent job board. Employers flag roles "open to adjacent talent," pay to post, and pay for skill-matched candidate access. In V0 the board runs manually as concierge introductions — Carlos reviews each employer's role, identifies 2-3 candidates with defensible skill overlap, and makes warm introductions with context — because a board without liquidity is a ghost town, and hand-matching is how the mechanic gets validated and priced before it becomes self-serve.

**Pricing (V0 concierge):** $500-2000 per qualified introduction, $5000-15000 per successful placement, depending on role seniority and employer size. These are provisional. Adjust after 3-5 real transactions produce actual price discovery.

**V1 (post-validation):** semi-automated matching — the platform surfaces the shortlist, Carlos still reviews before the intro. **V2: the board proper** — self-serve postings with the adjacent-open flag, paid placement tiers, candidate matching from the instrument's data. Each step only happens when the previous one proves demand; the destination is fixed, the pace is evidence-driven.

## KPI framework

**Leading indicators (monthly):**
- Unique architects engaging with instrument
- Reddit organic reach (comments + upvotes on tracked accounts)
- Route-page organic sessions from Google
- Email captures from report modal
- AEC educator conversations opened

**Lagging indicators (quarterly):**
- Employer introductions attempted
- Introductions converting to interviews
- Placements or paid pilots closed
- Revenue booked
- Recognizable brand mentions unprompted (Reddit, LinkedIn, press)

**Vanity metrics to ignore:**
- Total pageviews (traffic without qualified engagement is noise)
- Social media follower counts
- Newsletter subscribers without engagement rate context
- App downloads (there is no app)

## Anti-objectives

Things this project should explicitly *not* try to become:

- **A title-based job board.** The board PivotHop becomes is defined by the adjacent-open flag and skill matching; the moment it competes on posting volume with Indeed, it has lost its reason to exist.
- **A vertical-locked tool.** The product is global and profession-agnostic; verticals are marketing sequencing, never product boundaries. (This reverses an earlier position — the moat is the methodology and the adjacency data, not the niche.)
- **A hiring-diversity thought leadership brand.** The article on hiring adjacent talent is a strategic asset for reaching employers. It is not an identity to inhabit.
- **A LinkedIn influencer play for Carlos.** LinkedIn is a distribution channel, not an identity to build. If it starts pulling attention away from the product, cut back.
- **An AI-first product.** AI writes connective tissue in the report, not the substance. The substance is scraped data + Carlos's judgment + user's inputs.

## The one metric that matters most

**How many people, in the first 90 days, get to day 6 of engagement with the tool.** (The first cohort will be mostly the launch vertical — architects — because that's where the marketing points; the metric itself is profession-agnostic.)

Day 6 was chosen in §6 of the bible as the threshold that separates casual visitors from people who consider PivotHop part of their thinking. Everything else is downstream of this. If the tool can't get people to day 6, the future job board has no candidate supply. If it can, everything else becomes solvable in sequence.

Track this metric daily. Post it on the wall.
