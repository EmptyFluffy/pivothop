# PivotHop — Blog Strategy

*The editorial engine that builds topical authority around the instrument. Not a content marketing calendar. A publication with a point of view.*

---

## The role of the blog in the business

The blog is not a lead-generation funnel. It is a **credibility engine** that does three specific jobs:

1. **Ranks in Google for adjacent-to-instrument queries** — someone Googling "should I leave architecture" or "how transferable are architecture skills" lands on a substantive piece and, downstream, discovers the tool.
2. **Provides the sustained editorial voice** that makes PivotHop feel like a publication with a point of view, not a SaaS with a landing page and a nav.
3. **Feeds the Reddit and LinkedIn distribution channels** — each post becomes source material for shorter derivative content on those platforms.

**Zapier's blog produces 67% of their organic traffic.** For a niche AEC-adjacent business, the blog is potentially even more important, because the pillar articles are the only surface that can rank for the "why" queries that drive employer awareness.

## The five content pillars (from Notion bible)

Every piece of content maps to one of these five pillars. If a topic doesn't fit any pillar, it doesn't belong on the blog.

**1. What Carried Over**

The observed skill transfers from an origin profession to its adjacent fields. Data-heavy. Uses the scrape. The pillar is origin-agnostic; launch content instantiates it for architecture, and each new vertical gets its own instantiations of the same pillar.

Example titles:
- The 8 non-architecture skills that appear most often in postings hiring architects
- Why systems thinking is the most valuable thing architecture school teaches (and no one names it)
- The five skills that make architects hireable in product design (without a portfolio pivot)

**2. Run It 10,000 Times**

Probabilistic thinking about career decisions. Pushes back against motivational-poster career advice. Frames pivots as bets, not destinies.

Example titles:
- What actually happens when architects pivot to product design (from 12,000 job postings)
- The seven common architect exit paths, ranked by transition speed and salary trajectory
- Why the "follow your passion" advice fails architects specifically

**3. Career Half-Life**

The observation that most professional careers have specific durability windows. Architecture's is around 12-15 years before people either lean deep into partnership or exit. Uses this as the narrative frame for pivots being normal, not exceptional.

Example titles:
- The 12-year decision every architect faces
- Why architecture's career half-life is shorter than the profession admits
- What partnership actually costs: the numbers most senior architects don't share

**4. Shape of Work**

The observed differences between how architecture works and how adjacent fields work. Practical, granular, useful.

Example titles:
- What Figma does that Rhino doesn't (and what to unlearn from years of Rhino)
- The 30-day design sprint vs. the 3-year building cycle: what actually changes
- Why product designers ship on Fridays and architects don't

**5. Unbundle the Job**

Reframes "architect" as a bundle of separable skills, each of which has its own market elsewhere. This is the pillar that most directly supports the instrument's whole premise.

Example titles:
- The seven jobs inside "architect" (and where each one goes solo)
- Why the profession is unbundling and no one at the AIA wants to say it out loud
- The 90-day plan to test whether you actually miss architecture or just miss the drawing

## The pillar article (flagship)

**"The Business Case for Hiring Adjacent Talent"** — logged separately in the Content Pillars Notion page. This is the employer-facing flagship. It sits inside the "Unbundle the Job" pillar but is the specific piece meant to reach VPs of Talent and HR leaders.

Ship gate: only after the employer-side product surfaces skill-overlap data at the level the article promises. See the pillar candidate doc for full details.

## V0 blog scope: 3-5 posts, not 30

**The 30-post plan is V1.** For V0, ship:

1. One data post per pillar (5 total), each 1200-2000 words, each grounded in real scrape data with at least one uncopyable sentence per post
2. Publication cadence: 1-2 per month during V0 (months 1-3), building to weekly by month 6 if the format works

The reason not to ship 30 posts in V0: shipping 30 posts before the scrape produces defensible data means 30 posts with AI-fluff paragraphs, which is exactly the sitewide-penalty trap. Ship 5 excellent posts. Prove they rank. Then scale to the 30-post plan.

## The 30-post plan (V1)

Six posts per pillar × 5 pillars = 30 posts. Rotate through pillars weekly to avoid Google seeing us as narrow. Every 6th week, ship a data-heavy flagship in the "What Carried Over" pillar (these are the highest-value, hardest-to-write pieces).

Post types within each pillar:

- **Data post** (2 per pillar): grounded in scrape data. The uncopyable moat.
- **Editorial essay** (2 per pillar): Carlos's judgment on a specific question. Under 1500 words.
- **How-to** (1 per pillar): practical instruction. Longer, 2000-3000 words. These rank well.
- **Interview or profile** (1 per pillar): a real architect who made the specific pivot the pillar addresses. These build community and produce quotable content.

**No listicles. No AI-written posts. No "10 tips for" formulas.**

## Voice discipline for blog posts

Every voice rule from `01-style-direction.md` applies, plus specifics for long-form:

- Lead with a specific claim, not a warm-up paragraph
- Every post needs at least one number (from the scrape or from cited sources)
- Every post needs at least one "here's what most people miss" moment
- Every post ends with a concrete next step or a question the reader can act on
- No "In this post, we'll cover..." opening. Get in and get to the point.
- No "Conclusion" heading. End with the actual ending.

## SEO structural requirements per post

- Original H1 (never the same as an existing post)
- Meta description written by hand (not auto-generated from opening paragraph)
- Author byline pointing to about page (E-E-A-T)
- One relevant preloaded route page linked in the body (drives instrument usage)
- One related pillar or post linked in the body (topic cluster)
- Structured data: Article schema with Carlos as author
- OG image: title over the design system's paper background, cobalt accent
- Publish date + last updated date visible on the page

## Distribution per post

Every post gets:

1. **A LinkedIn adaptation** — Carlos posts a 3-4 paragraph version in his own voice with a link to the full piece. Timed for weekday morning US or evening US.
2. **A Reddit consideration** — if the post directly answers a query that comes up in tracked subs, wait for a natural thread to land it in as Register 2 context. Never link-drop a post cold.
3. **A newsletter mention** — once the newsletter exists (V1), each post gets a summary + link in the following newsletter.
4. **Cross-linking from at least 2 existing posts** — every new post triggers editing 2 old posts to link to the new one.

## What's not the blog's job

- Answer FAQ (that's a FAQ page)
- Be an entrepreneurial diary of Carlos's building process (that's a different project, and it should live on a different domain if it exists at all)
- Rank for competitive commercial keywords like "best career change tool" (those are the /routes/ pages' job)
- Interview famous industry figures for credibility-borrowing (this is a common blog trap — it borrows credibility temporarily without building any)
- Announce product features (product updates live on a changelog page, not the blog)

## Content maintenance

**Every 6 months:** review all published posts. Update the ones with dated statistics, republish under the "last updated" date. This is a real ranking signal in 2026.

**Every 12 months:** kill the bottom 20% of posts that never ranked or converted. Update their URLs to redirect to the closest relevant piece. Google rewards sites that prune, not sites that hoard.

## The one uncopyable move

The blog succeeds if it's the only publication in the AEC-career space that consistently:

- Publishes grounded, numbered, honest arguments
- Refuses the motivational vocabulary
- Speaks like a working architect, not a marketing person
- Rejects the incentives (no bootcamp affiliate revenue, no LinkedIn Learning partnerships, no sponsored posts)

That combination is a moat. Every other publication in the space is structurally incapable of doing all four. That's why they'll keep losing readers to us.
