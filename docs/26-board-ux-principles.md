# 26 — Board UX principles

*What makes a job board feel decent to use, and which of it we adopt. Written from a read of HiringCafe (2026-07-28), the one product job seekers describe as pleasant.*

---

## The diagnosis

Job search feels hostile because of dishonesty and noise, not scarcity. There are plenty of listings. What exhausts people is ghost jobs, reposts of reposts, "competitive salary", recruiter spam, login walls, and applications that vanish.

HiringCafe did not add delight. It **removed the adversarial layer**. Four moves, all subtractive:

1. **Provenance.** It indexes company career pages directly, so a listing dies when the company kills it. This is the ghost-job fix, and it is structural rather than a filter.
2. **Salary up front.** Stated pay sits on the card, not buried in paragraph nine.
3. **Filters precise enough to return few results.** Comp floor, remote-ness, experience level, company size, boolean. Fewer-but-right reads as respect for the reader's time.
4. **Triage without opening.** A per-posting summary of requirements and tools, so the reader decides from the card.

Underneath all four is one posture: **the job seeker is the customer, not the inventory.** Most boards sell seeker attention to employers, and it is legible in every interaction.

## What we adopt

We already share the posture: source boards only, no LinkedIn or Indeed, honest empty states, numbers over adjectives. The gap is that none of it is visible to a visitor.

**1. Say the provenance out loud.** *(cheapest, highest trust-per-hour)*
We have `first-seen.json`, we drop ~20k duplicates per run, and we pull straight from Greenhouse, Lever, and Ashby. A visitor sees none of this. Put it on the card — "direct from the company's board · first seen 3 days ago" — and a standing line on `/jobs`: no LinkedIn, no reposts, no ghost jobs. The data exists; only the sentence is missing.

**2. Ship the filter nobody else can build.**
Their filters are precise. Ours can be categorically different, because we hold readiness: *only roles I am 60%+ ready for*, *ready in under six months*, *pays more than I earn now*. HiringCafe answers "what matches my title." We answer "what my skills reach when the title does not match." Readiness-as-a-filter is how the graph becomes a product feature instead of a visualization.

**3. Make the card triage-able.**
Match percentage, salary band, skill chips with their marks, and the one line that closes it: "you have 7 of these 9 — missing Revit and ISO 19650." Their version is generated by a model. Ours is computed and defensible, which is the whole point.

**4. Finish the saved search.**
The capture band already offers "email me when the board adds roles my skills already reach." That is their saved-high-signal-search behaviour, half built. Wiring it is what turns a one-visit tool into a returning one.

**5. Group by company.** Cards grouped by employer cut repetition when one company posts nine near-identical roles. Cheap, and it removes a specific irritation.

## What we do not copy

Reviewers consistently report HiringCafe as slow. Our statically generated pages are a real edge and are not worth trading for interactivity we do not need.

We also do not copy the model-written posting summary. Ours must stay computed — the numbers are the product, and a generated paraphrase is exactly the uncheckable content the instrument exists to replace.
