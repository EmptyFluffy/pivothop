# PivotHop — About Page

*Not marketing copy. A biographical document that carries E-E-A-T weight, positions the brand, and answers the question every serious visitor asks: "who built this and why."*

---

## Why the about page is structural, not decorative

Most about pages are decorative. Ours isn't. It does three specific jobs:

1. **E-E-A-T authority signal for Google.** Every route page, every article, every report inherits credibility from Carlos's biographical page. Google's 2026 quality algorithm rewards visible, verifiable expertise attached to author bylines. Without this page, PivotHop is anonymous. With this page, PivotHop is the tool built by a working architect.
2. **Positioning proof.** Every claim the brand makes about honesty, industry insider knowledge, and rejection of category norms — this page is where those claims get evidence. Carlos's story *is* the argument.
3. **Trust conversion for serious visitors.** Employers about to invest in a concierge introduction, journalists writing about the tool, AEC educators considering a partnership — they all read this page first. It converts skepticism to trust or it doesn't.

## Structure (top to bottom)

### 1. Eyebrow + title

`ABOUT` (mono uppercase eyebrow)

`Built by an architect who made the pivot.` (h1, Space Grotesk 500, sentence case)

Not: "Meet the founder!" Not: "Our story." Not: "Why we started PivotHop."

### 2. The lead paragraph

3-4 sentences that establish the whole thesis in the first thing anyone reads. Draft:

> Carlos is a Costa Rican architect. He trained at ETSAM Madrid, spent years designing luxury hospitality projects at Gensler, Zaha Hadid Architects, and Antoine Predock, and made the specific pivot most of his colleagues quietly wanted to make: from drawing plans to writing the code that generates them. He built PivotHop because the tool he needed when he was pivoting didn't exist — for architects or for anyone else. It still doesn't. So he's making it, for every profession whose skills travel further than its title.

### 3. The Portrait

Real photograph. Textured, not corporate. Ideally shot in a working context — at a workstation, at a project site, at a whiteboard — not a smiling headshot against a beige wall. Landscape orientation. Full-bleed to the column edge, treated with the same visual restraint as the rest of the design system.

Caption underneath in mono: `SAN JOSÉ · JULY 2026` or similar. Just the location and date. No name — the surrounding copy carries that.

### 4. The story (3-4 short sections)

Written as prose, not bullets. Each section 2-4 sentences. Section headers in Space Grotesk 600. The system is two typefaces only — Space Grotesk for words, Space Mono for data. There is no third face.

**Section: The training**

The ETSAM years, the reasons for choosing architecture, the honest moments where it didn't fit what was promised. Something like: "Architecture school teaches you to see space. It doesn't teach you what to do with the seeing. Most of us figured that out in the ten years after graduating."

**Section: The offices**

Gensler, ZHA, Predock. What Carlos actually learned in each, told without name-dropping. The specific projects that mattered (Papagayo, Four Seasons Mecca competition, etc). The moment where computational tools started to matter more than trace paper.

**Section: The pivot**

Carlos's own path from architecture into computational design and technology. Told with specifics: which tools, which projects, which conversations. This section is proof of concept for what the whole tool is trying to help other architects do. It should be honest about what was hard.

**Section: Why PivotHop exists**

The tool this project is trying to be, and why it needs to exist. Rejection of category norms — not a bootcamp funnel, not an AI career coach. Just the numbers, honestly. This is where the brand voice from `01-style-direction.md` gets to fully speak.

### 5. What Carlos doesn't do

A short section signaling boundaries and taste. Something like:

> Carlos does not sell bootcamp affiliate content. Does not recommend courses he hasn't personally taken. Does not accept sponsored content from career-tech companies. Does not use AI-generated writing on this site. Does not have investors he needs to grow for. This is a small business with one full-time person and a clear purpose. It is not a startup.

### 6. Credentials strip

A quiet, non-bragging strip of facts:

- CFIA-registered architect (Costa Rica)
- ETSAM Madrid, Master's in Architecture
- Senior Project Designer at Predock (current)
- Prior: Gensler, ZHA collaboration, ICT Costa Rica master plan work
- Independent practice AVARQ
- Bass player in ADULTHOOD (shoegaze, San José)

The last item is deliberate. It says "I am a whole person" without needing to explain.

### 7. Contact

Simple and human:

- Email: carlos@pivothop.com (or wherever)
- LinkedIn: [profile URL]
- If you're an employer interested in hand-matched candidates: [link to the employer page or a dedicated contact form]
- If you're an architect thinking about pivoting and want to talk: [same email, different intent — Carlos personally reads and replies to these]

The last line about direct email is important. It says the founder is reachable. This is a differentiator in a category dominated by faceless SaaS platforms.

## Voice discipline for this page

Every voice rule from `01-style-direction.md` applies, plus:

- **First person or third person? Third.** "Carlos is..." not "I am..." Third person reads as biographical documentation, not personal blog. It's more credible and it ages better.
- **Named specifics beat abstractions.** "Zaha Hadid Architects" beats "prestigious international firms." "The Papagayo project" beats "high-end hospitality projects."
- **Small honest details beat sweeping claims.** "The moment where computational tools started to matter more than trace paper" is a specific mental image. "I discovered my passion for technology" is category-standard cliché.
- **No corporate biography words.** No "seasoned," no "passionate," no "extensive experience," no "proven track record," no "results-driven," no "creative problem-solver."

## What the about page must not do

- Include a mission statement written by an AI or a marketing consultant
- List "core values" as bulleted virtues
- Try to be inspiring
- Explain the rabbit logo
- Include stock photos of team members who don't exist
- Fake testimonials or made-up quotes
- "I'm currently open to opportunities" language
- A "What I'm working on now" section that gets stale in three weeks

## Length target

600-900 words of prose. Long enough to build real credibility, short enough that a serious visitor will actually read it. Under 500 words feels thin. Over 1200 feels self-indulgent.

## SEO signal

- Author schema (JSON-LD) with Carlos as sameAs LinkedIn, sameAs professional profile, sameAs Predock page if possible
- Meta description: a version of the lead paragraph
- OG image: the portrait, cropped to social share ratio

## Update cadence

The about page is a living document. Update when:

- Carlos's professional situation meaningfully changes (leaves Predock, opens new AVARQ chapter, launches a book, etc.)
- The PivotHop story reaches a milestone worth including (first paid placement, first press coverage, first major partnership)
- Something in the current version stops being true

Do not update to add:
- Vanity metrics ("Now used by 10,000 architects!")
- Awards or accolades unless they materially matter
- New "team members" if there aren't any
- Anything that adds length without adding trust
